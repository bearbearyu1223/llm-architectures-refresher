2. Why divide by sqrt(d_k)?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  d_k   logit std  max w (raw)  H (raw)  max w (/sqrt)  H (/sqrt)
  -----------------------------------------------------------------
  4        1.9642       0.5222   1.2986         0.3401     1.7548
  16       3.9493       0.7428   0.6825         0.3523     1.7350
  64       7.9399       0.8738   0.3223         0.3506     1.7407
  256     15.9159       0.9326   0.1691         0.3533     1.7318
  1024    32.4533       0.9735   0.0674         0.3701     1.7103

  uniform-over-8 entropy would be ln(8) = 2.0794

  >> Unscaled, attention collapses to a hard argmax as d_k grows. Scaled, the entropy barely moves — that is the entire point of 1/sqrt(d_k).

        key0    key1    key2    key3    key4    key5

  q5    0.126    0.348    0.240    0.160    0.040    0.086    

  mass on future positions           0.0000
  each row sums to 1                 yes

  >> Zero mass above the diagonal is what makes the model autoregressive: token i's representation cannot depend on token i+1.

4. RoPE: absolute rotation, relative score
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  same q, k vectors; same offset (3); different absolute positions:

  query pos m  key pos n  offset m-n  q_m . k_n
  -----------------------------------------------
  0                    3          -3    -1.1905
  5                    8          -3    -1.1905
  105                108          -3    -1.1905
  4093              4096          -3    -1.1905

  spread across absolute positions   1.073e-06

  now vary the offset instead, holding the query at position 0:

  offset  q_0 . k_offset
  ------------------------
  0              -0.0071
  1               0.0917
  2              -0.4322
  4              -1.5648
  8               2.2522
  32             -0.2377
  128             9.3205

  >> Rotating by absolute position makes the dot product a function of the difference of angles — relative position falls out of the geometry, with no learned position embedding and no extra parameters.
